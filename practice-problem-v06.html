Iran's Transit Tax on Oil Through the Strait of Hormuz

Iran controls the Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly 20% of global oil passes. Facing fiscal pressure, Iran imposes a transit fee of $40 per unit on all oil shipped through the strait — effectively an excise tax falling on suppliers.

Given
Demand: P = 200 − 2Q
Supply: P = 20 + 2Q
Transit fee: t = $40 per unit

Demand has a negative slope — it falls from top-left to bottom-right. Supply has a positive slope — it rises from bottom-left to top-right. The intercept is where the curve crosses the price axis (Q = 0).

Summary

Pre-tax: Q* = 45, P* = 110. Total surplus = 4,050.

Post-tax (t = $40): Q*ₜ = 35, Pc = 130, Pp = 90. The burden is shared equally — consumers pay $20 more, producers receive $20 less — because supply and demand slopes are equal.

Tax revenue = 1,400. Deadweight loss = 200. Total surplus falls by exactly the DWL.
