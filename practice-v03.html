Oil Markets & the Strait of Hormuz

The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway through which roughly 20% of global oil passes. A geopolitical crisis closes the strait. Simultaneously, the Canadian federal government has imposed an excise tax on oil producers.

Given
Demand: P = 200 − 2Q
Supply: P = 20 + 2Q
Excise tax: t = $40 per unit

5
Calculate the welfare effects
▾
5

Using the pre- and post-tax equilibria, calculate each welfare area using triangle and rectangle formulas.

CS = ½(200 − Pc) × Q*ₜ  ·  PS = ½(Pp − 20) × Q*ₜ  ·  TR = t × Q*ₜ  ·  DWL = ½ × t × (Q* − Q*ₜ)
// CS = ½ × (200 − 130) × 35
CS = ½ × 70 × 35 = 1,225
// PS = ½ × (90 − 20) × 35
PS = ½ × 70 × 35 = 1,225
// Tax revenue = 40 × 35
TR = 1,400
// DWL = ½ × 40 × (45 − 35)
DWL = ½ × 40 × 10 = 200
// Post-tax total surplus
TS = 1,225 + 1,225 + 1,400 = 3,850
// Pre-tax: ½ × (200 − 20) × 45
TS₀ = ½ × 180 × 45 = 4,050
// Loss = DWL = 4,050 − 3,850 = 200 ✓

Summary

Pre-tax equilibrium: Q* = 45, P* = 110. Total surplus = 4,050.

Post-tax (t = $40): Q*ₜ = 35, Pc = 130, Pp = 90. The $40 tax burden is shared equally — consumers pay $20 more, producers receive $20 less — because supply and demand have equal slopes.

Tax revenue = 1,400. Deadweight loss = 200. Total surplus falls by exactly the DWL — the permanent efficiency cost of the tax.
